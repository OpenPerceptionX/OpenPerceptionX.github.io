01At a glance

Teach robots tool use through object motion

P2P-T turns unstructured human videos into structured, object-centric pose trajectories. A foundation-model pipeline finds the tool, reconstructs its geometry, and tracks its 6D motion. A two-stage learner then transfers those priors to a low-level robot policy, keeping the task affordance while leaving embodiment-specific control to the robot.

Automated pose extraction

Grounded-SAM, SAM 3D, and FoundationPose convert raw RGB video into clean tool masks, meshes, and 6D trajectories.

02

Embodiment-independent priors

The Stage 1 world model predicts future object poses, so the learned dynamics transfer across human and robot bodies.

03

Pose-aware execution

Stage 2 injects future pose latents into a diffusion action expert for precise, receding-horizon control.

02Method

Two stages bridge pixels and physical action

Stage 1

Object-centric world model

Frozen DINOv2, SigLIP, and T5 encoders feed an autoregressive transformer that predicts future 6D tool poses and auxiliary frames from human demonstrations.

Stage 2

Pose-aware post-training

A frozen Stage 1 model produces future pose latents. An RDT-based action expert conditions on those latents, language, images, and proprioception.

Deployment

Receding-horizon control

The robot samples action chunks and refreshes its pose prior as new camera observations arrive, connecting object-level dynamics with robot-specific control.

03Robot demonstrations

Each task requires continuous spatial reasoning, contact-rich control, and a tool-specific working pose. Tools are randomly placed, then re-oriented and operated by a UR10e robot with a Robotiq 2F-140 gripper.

04Evaluation protocol

Trajectory inference from random tool poses

For each task, we randomly place the tool on the table, requiring the robot to adapt to its initial position and pose, infer the corresponding trajectory, and progressively reorient the tool into the correct working pose while maintaining contact or precise directional control. This evaluates tool use from realistic, unaligned starting conditions rather than simplified, pre-aligned setups. We use 100 demonstrations for adaptation and evaluate each task over 50 trials.

06Qualitative analysis

Structured priors carry tool affordances across bodies

Failure cases

Why pose priors matter

In most cases, when tools are placed in initial poses that were not seen during training, baseline models linger around the target and cannot complete a reliable grasp. In other cases, they overfit to a demonstrated trajectory, grasp a random component of the tool, and fail to use the tool correctly. P2P-T's future pose latents make the intended orientation and progression explicit, helping the action expert maintain a stable working trajectory.
